# Anonymized example: a technical RL sharing session

This is an illustrative **process case study**, not a transcript, benchmark, or endorsement by any employer.

## Starting point

- Goal: introduce RL to an audience already familiar with deep learning and LLMs.
- Time: roughly one hour.
- First outline: why RL → math → optimization → LLM training pipeline → infrastructure.
- Early issue: technically complete written notes did not supply a natural oral narrative.

## How the design improved

The talk adopted one central question: **How can a trajectory change model parameters, and what infrastructure is needed to produce many such trajectories?** It used a tiny grid-world example for formal concepts, a small group-reward example for the algorithm bridge, and a generic coding-agent example for the training pipeline.

The most consequential design change was to write the oral narrative and then **align** it with the projected tutorial. A stable `[P00]–[P25]` map tied every displayed section to one matching speaker note and expected time. The result was a more coherent session; late material may still be skipped when the question budget or earlier explanation consumes the hour.

## Transferable lessons

- A concept map is a knowledge store, not necessarily an oral syllabus.
- A readable speaker script is a test of narrative coherence, not just a performance prop.
- The live projection must stand on its own because the speaker may not read separate notes.
- Link reliability and rendering are part of delivery quality.
- One hour of speaking does not alone establish audience learning; gather questions and feedback after the talk.
